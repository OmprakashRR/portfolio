Worked example · Summer heat gain

24°C inside.
30°C outside.

Sunlight enters through exposed glass while warmer outdoor conditions also supply heat. Calculate the room’s temperature after the first ten minutes.

Assumptions

Initial indoor temperature = 24°C
Outdoor temperature = 30°C
Room heat-loss coefficient, H = 40 W/°C
Effective room heat capacity, C = 3,000 kJ/°C
Time period = 10 minutes = 600 seconds

H and C are assumed teaching values. C includes thermally active building materials and contents, not just the air. No other heat gains or cooling are included.

1. Solar heat through the exposed glass

Exposed height = 0.43 m
Window width = 1.5 m
Solar radiation on the window plane = 700 W/m²
Glass solar gain factor, g = 0.60

Exposed area = 0.43 × 1.5 = 0.645 m²
Solar heat gain = area × radiation × g
Solar heat gain = 0.645 × 700 × 0.60 = 270.9 W

The glazing admits a fraction of the incident solar energy. Watts measure the rate of heat gain, not an amount of stored energy.

2. Heat from the warmer outdoors

Outdoor heat gain = H × (Tout − Tin)
Outdoor heat gain = 40 × (30 − 24) = 240 W

The sign is positive: outdoors is warmer than indoors, so this heat flow is into the room.

3. Net energy stored in ten minutes

Total heat gain = 270.9 + 240 = 510.9 W
Energy gained = power × time ÷ 1,000
Energy gained = 510.9 × 600 ÷ 1,000 = 306.54 kJ

4. Indoor temperature change

Temperature rise = energy gained ÷ heat capacity
Temperature rise = 306.54 ÷ 3,000 = 0.10218°C
Indoor temperature after ten minutes ≈ 24.10°C

Compare: block the solar gain completely

Only the initial 240 W gain from the warmer outdoors remains.

Temperature rise = (240 × 600 ÷ 1,000) ÷ 3,000
Temperature rise = 0.048°C
Indoor temperature after ten minutes ≈ 24.05°C

Shading reduces warming, but the warmer outdoors still supplies heat. Greater thermal mass slows the temperature rise.

What this calculation assumes

This short-step estimate holds heat-flow rates at their starting values. The room warms gradually; the result is not an instant equilibrium temperature. Solar gain through the shaded portion of the glass is ignored in this simplified example.

The interactive activities use their own stated inputs. Their values need not match this separate board example.
