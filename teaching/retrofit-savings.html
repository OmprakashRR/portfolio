2. Exposed areas and U-values

How U relates to resistance and construction year

For a uniform added layer, R = thickness in metres ÷ conductivity. Existing whole-element R already includes its relevant surface terms. This simplified update excludes new fixings, repeating bridges, gaps and changes to other layers. It does not establish suitability for cavity fill.

Age alone cannot establish the actual U-value. Start with assessment data or construction evidence. If roof insulation is unknown, historical DEAP roof age defaults include 0.49 for 1978–1993, 0.40 for 1994–1999 and 0.36 for 2000–2004. Use the current SEAI DEAP guide, and check extensions or upgrades separately.

3. Heat loss now, and energy over a year

Annual heat saved (kWh) = (U before − U after) × area × degree days × 24 ÷ 1,000
Annual gas-cost saving (€) = heat saved ÷ boiler efficiency × gas rate

Where do the 2,000 degree days and 48 come from?

With an assumed heating balance temperature of 15.5°C, a day averaging 5.5°C contributes approximately 10 degree days. In our imaginary year, 200 such days contribute 2,000 degree days and the other 165 days contribute zero. This is a teaching scenario, not weather evidence.

2,000 degree days × 24 hours/day ÷ 1,000 Wh/kWh = 48. That is the annual shortcut in the slides. Replace 2,000 with a representative local total, using the stated balance temperature. Met Éireann publishes degree days below 15.5°C in its Dublin Airport monthly dataset. Sum all 12 months of a year or use a long-run average. Published degree-day methods may use more detailed daily temperature profiles.

The heating balance temperature is the outside threshold below which heating is needed after average internal and solar gains. It is different from the indoor setpoint and varies by home. Do not multiply degree days by the separate snapshot temperature gap or by another annual heating-hours figure.

4. Grants and upfront cost

For an individual-grant project, obtain the grant offer before starting. Insulation and window grants generally require a home built and occupied before 2011. Window support has completed-home insulation/HLI and product conditions. Apartment roof grants need an exposed heat-loss roof. Construction suitability, ventilation and any management-company permissions still matter.

5. A heat pump supplies the remaining heat

Heat-pump investment and grant assumptions

For houses, the water-based bundle can include up to €10,500 system support, €2,000 for qualifying central-heating work and €2,000 boiler scrappage. Components have separate conditions. Apartment maximum is €11,500. Existing heat-pump replacement and air-to-air systems use different limits. The maximum bundle is not an automatic grant.

Homes must generally have been built and occupied before 2021. Pre-2007 homes may need a technical assessment unless a qualifying valid BER permits the self-declaration route. Check SEAI assessment and grant rules. A screening calculation cannot size a heat pump or confirm flow temperatures, emitter suitability or achieved seasonal performance.

What this estimate leaves out

This is a steady-state transmission and degree-day comparison, not a BER or dynamic simulation. Floors, doors, air leakage, junctions, window solar-gain changes, hot water and distribution losses are outside the component calculation. The balance-temperature assumption approximates gains. Schedules, occupants heating more after an upgrade and actual weather can change bill savings. Standing charges remain unchanged. Simple payback excludes finance, maintenance and changing tariffs. Do not add a separate insulation saving again when comparing the heat pump with the already-insulated boiler.

Check the magnitude against your own space-heating energy. If the predicted component saving exceeds baseline useful heat, review the inputs rather than treat the result as a saving. Proper ventilation remains necessary. Use this to ask informed questions and compare first estimates before committing to work.
